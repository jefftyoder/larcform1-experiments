    \section{larcform1 progress report --- supercooled Arctic liquid in the
ClimaAtmos
SCM}\label{larcform1-progress-report-supercooled-arctic-liquid-in-the-climaatmos-scm}

\textbf{Date:} 2026-07-05 · \textbf{Case:} Larcform1 (Pithan et al.~2016
Arctic winter single-column intercomparison) · \textbf{Model:}
ClimaAtmos.jl 1M prognostic-EDMFx column, slab surface @ 250 K ·
\textbf{Reference:} EC-Earth from the Pithan 2016 intercomparison
dataset

\subsection{Executive summary}\label{executive-summary}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Problem.} Our ClimaAtmos implementation of the Larcform1 case
  produced \emph{identically zero} supercooled cloud liquid
  (\texttt{clw}), where the reference model (EC-Earth) sustains a
  mixed-phase cloud for its first two days.
\item
  \textbf{Root cause found and confirmed} (clw sensitivity experiment,
  12 variations): the default \texttt{ConstantTimescale} vapor→cloud-ice
  deposition is an \emph{unbounded} Wegener--Bergeron--Findeisen (WBF)
  sink --- with a 100 s timescale and no dependence on ice mass or
  ice-nucleating-particle (INP) availability, it pins vapor at ice
  saturation so the liquid-condensation gate never opens. Switching to
  \texttt{cloud\_ice\_formation:\ "TemperatureDependent"} (Frostenberg
  2023 INP-limited deposition, variation v10) restores the cloud at the
  right height, temperature, and onset time.
\item
  \textbf{A 16--40× faster forward model was validated} (SCM speed
  tests, s1--s13): the dominant cost of the original config was
  10-minute-cadence debugging diagnostics (\textasciitilde90\% of
  runtime), not physics; a convergence ladder showed the 100-level
  stretched grid (10 m at the surface, 5 km top) is the \emph{converged}
  answer; the adopted calibration config (s11) solves 2 simulated days
  in \textasciitilde42 s and preserves the v1-physics discriminator (clw
  still exactly zero --- the WBF failure is physics, not resolution).
\item
  \textbf{Six microphysics parameters were calibrated} with unscented
  Kalman inversion (UKI, 6 iterations × 13 members, 54 min total)
  against EC-Earth day-2 clw/cli profiles. This required un-deadening
  the Frostenberg 2023 \texttt{a}/\texttt{b} INP-curve coefficients,
  which ClimaParams defines but CloudMicrophysics (v0.36 \emph{and}
  upstream main) never reads --- fixed via a one-function vendored
  patch. Day-2 misfit fell from χ²/dim 12.8 to \textbf{1.73}.
\item
  \textbf{Out-of-sample verification passed.} In 5-day runs, the
  calibrated model reproduces EC-Earth's \emph{entire cloud life cycle}
  --- including glaciation at hour 54 (EC-Earth: 51) that was never part
  of the training objective; the uncalibrated baseline holds 5× too much
  liquid and glaciates a day late.
\item
  \textbf{Data discovery en route:} the EC-Earth reference record is 20
  days (481 hourly steps), not 2 as all pre-2026-07-05 analyses assumed
  --- and EC-Earth's own cloud glaciates on day 3. The day-2 calibration
  window targets the transient mixed-phase phase; neither model
  reproduces the multi-day persistence of \emph{observed} Arctic clouds,
  which is now a documented physics question separate from tuning.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Where things stand:} calibration validated, final parameter
means adopted, all work committed
(\texttt{experiments/clw\ calibration}, vendored
\texttt{CloudMicrophysics.jl} patch, experiment write-ups); the
ClimaAtmos fork branch \texttt{jy/coldslab} is pushed.

    \subsection{1. The target: EC-Earth's Larcform1 cloud --- and what its
full record
shows}\label{the-target-ec-earths-larcform1-cloud-and-what-its-full-record-shows}

The Larcform1 case is a polar-night, single-column Arctic experiment: a
250 K atmosphere over a freezing surface, no sun, physics left to build
a cloud. Among the Pithan 2016 intercomparison models, EC-Earth is the
closest match to our setup (it, too, runs ``no snow, fixed ice'' ---
Table 3) and the one whose modest liquid water path (day-2 mean clwvi ≈
0.038 kg/m², matching Table 5's 0.037) sits at the \emph{low},
observation-adjacent end of the intercomparison spread (ECHAM-HAM: 0.39;
the paper notes model LWPs generally run high vs SHEBA observations).
That makes it a good calibration target.

A caveat discovered only on 2026-07-05: \texttt{EC-Earth.nc} spans
\textbf{20 days}, not 2 --- every earlier analysis sliced the first 48
h. The full record (below) shows the mixed-phase cloud is
\emph{transient in EC-Earth too}: liquid collapses on day 3 (WBF
glaciation), ice peaks on day 4 and slowly sediments away. The day-2
calibration window therefore targets the transient mixed-phase phase,
and ``persistence'' comparisons only apply within days 1--2.

\textbf{Scope note on the surface:} we use a slab ocean (heat capacity,
no ice/snow conduction), standing in for the protocol's interactive 1 m
ice + 0.1 m snow. The paper's clear-state result (surface-based
inversions, which hinge on snow insulation) is out of scope for this
surface; only the cloudy state --- our target --- is meaningfully
comparable, and EC-Earth shares that limitation.

\textbf{Mechanism.} In the 1M scheme, liquid condenses only when vapor
exceeds \emph{liquid} saturation, but the default ice scheme deposits
vapor toward \emph{ice} saturation on a fixed 100 s timescale with no
dependence on existing ice mass or INP availability. Below freezing, ice
saturation \textless{} liquid saturation always, so vapor is pinned
below the liquid gate: WBF with the physical brake (finite ice surface
area) removed → clw ≡ 0. The \texttt{TemperatureDependent} alternative
computes the deposition timescale from diffusional growth onto
INP-nucleated crystals (Frostenberg 2023 Arctic INP climatology). At
this cloud's −10 °C there are \textasciitilde1 INP m⁻³ --- deposition
starts \textasciitilde10⁷× slower, liquid wins the vapor competition,
and ice then bootstraps as crystals grow (v11/v12 confirm the
\emph{feedback}, not the timescale value, is what matters).

This finding was later hardened by the speed-test control \textbf{s13}:
v1 physics on a converged 10 m grid still gives clw = 0.0 at every level
and hour --- no amount of vertical resolution rescues the liquid. (The
literature counterpoint is instructive: Pithan 2016's WRF-200l lost its
liquid at \emph{high} resolution via 1.2 m-level frost drying --- a
different mechanism our 10 m grids don't exhibit.)

Calibration needs \textasciitilde80+ forward runs; the original config
took \textasciitilde11--12 min each. The speed-test experiment
(\texttt{experiments/scm\ speed\ tests/}, runs s1--s13) found:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Diagnostics, not physics, dominated runtime.} The 54-variable,
  10-minute-cadence EDMF/microphysics-tendency groups (accumulated
  averages, computed every timestep) consumed \textasciitilde90\% of the
  original runtime. Trimming to the 20-variable converter set alone
  brought a full-grid run to 26.5 s.
\item
  \textbf{The stretched grid is the converged answer, not a landmine.} A
  uniform-resolution ladder (167 m → 100 → 50 → 25 → 10 m) marches
  monotonically toward the tanh-stretched grid's behavior (onset h5→h0,
  persistence 40→48 of 48 h). The 100-stretched-level grid (s10) matches
  the 500-level uniform 10 m anchor (s9) at ⅓ its cost. The config's
  z-banding warning did not materialize under v10 physics;
  onset/persistence gaps vs EC-Earth were a resolution artifact, not
  microphysics.
\item
  \textbf{dt\_rad 30 min is safe} (polar night, LW only): rlds shifts
  −0.4 W/m²; 1 h was marginal and rejected.
\item
  \textbf{s13 control:} the light grid preserves the v1-physics
  discriminator (clw exactly 0.0) --- see §2.
\end{itemize}

levels (10 m at the surface), dt 30 s, dt\_rad 30 min, minimal

    \subsection{4. Calibration: 6-parameter UKI against EC-Earth day-2
profiles}\label{calibration-6-parameter-uki-against-ec-earth-day-2-profiles}

\textbf{Setup} (\texttt{experiments/clw\ calibration/}): ClimaCalibrate
+ EnsembleKalmanProcesses \texttt{TransformUnscented} (UKI,
\texttt{impose\_prior=true}) → 13 deterministic sigma-point members per
iteration, run sequentially in one Julia process (JIT paid once;
\textasciitilde38 s/member, \textasciitilde8.2 min/iteration, 6
iterations + fixed cost = \textbf{54 min measured}).

\textbf{Observations:} EC-Earth day-2 (hours 25--48) time-mean clw and
cli profiles, interpolated to 10 pressure levels (995→905 hPa),
transformed \texttt{log10(q\ +\ 10⁻⁷)}; 20-dim observation vector,
diagonal noise σ = 0.3 log₁₀ units (≈ factor-2 per level). Target
cross-validated against Pithan 2016 Table 5.

\textbf{The dead-knob discovery.} The headline parameters ---
Frostenberg 2023 INP-curve coefficients \texttt{a}, \texttt{b} ---
turned out to be \emph{dead} in CloudMicrophysics: ClimaParams defines
them, the parameter struct stores them, but
\texttt{INP\_concentration\_mean} hardcodes the curve (registered v0.36
\textbf{and} upstream main, checked 2026-07-05). Calibrating them would
have been a silent no-op. Fixed with a one-function vendored patch
(\texttt{CloudMicrophysics.jl/} at repo root, wired into only the
calibration environment): the INP concentration becomes
\texttt{9·log(−b·T\_C/10)\ −\ log(a)} per the paper's Eq. (1), reducing
exactly to stock at a = b = 1. Verified analytically and via sigma-point
smoke tests before the full run. \emph{Candidate upstream contribution.}

    \subsection{5. The decisive test: 5-day out-of-sample
verification}\label{the-decisive-test-5-day-out-of-sample-verification}

The calibration only ever saw a day-2 snapshot --- and it boosted ice
production \textasciitilde4--5×, raising an obvious worry: does the
calibrated model now glaciate \emph{too readily} after day 2? Three
standalone 5-day runs answer this (final mean = ``calibrated'';
iteration-6 mean; stock-v10 ``baseline'').

\textbf{The calibrated model reproduces EC-Earth's full cloud life
cycle}: liquid buildup at the right magnitude on day 1, glaciation on
day 3 within 3 hours of EC-Earth's, ice-only aftermath. The glaciation
timing was never in the objective --- it \emph{emerged} from the
calibrated ice physics, which is strong evidence the parameters capture
mechanism rather than curve-fit. The baseline, by contrast, carries 5×
too much liquid through day 2 and glaciates a day late. (A corollary:
stock v10's celebrated ``48/48-hour persistence'' in the 2-day screen
was a record-length illusion --- it also glaciates, on day 4.)

    \subsection{6. Open questions and next
steps}\label{open-questions-and-next-steps}

\textbf{Candidate follow-ups} (recorded in the calibration write-up, not
yet started):

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{More UKI iterations} --- the misfit was still descending at
  iteration 6 (χ²/dim 1.73 \textgreater{} 1 means we're not yet inside
  the assumed noise).
\item
  \textbf{Add day-3+ clivi to the observation vector} --- now that the
  20-day record is understood, the ice life cycle (peak + decay) is
  fittable and directly targets the largest residual bias.
\item
  \textbf{Revisit the noise model} --- σ = 0.3 uniform in log₁₀ was a
  first guess; per-variable σ would let the ice phase pull harder.
\item
  \textbf{Upstream contributions to CliMA}: (a) the CloudMicrophysics
  Frostenberg \texttt{a}/\texttt{b} dead-knob patch; (b) the finding
  that default \texttt{ConstantTimescale} deposition makes sustained
  mixed-phase Arctic clouds impossible in 1M runs (plus a known wrong
  \texttt{clivi} docstring).
\item
  \textbf{The physics question beyond tuning}: neither EC-Earth nor the
  calibrated model sustains supercooled liquid past day 3, but
  \emph{observed} Arctic mixed-phase clouds famously persist. Whether
  ClimaAtmos can be configured to do so (INP recycling? entrainment of
  INP-depleted air?) is a genuinely open --- and interesting ---
  question this tuned baseline is now well-positioned to explore.
\end{enumerate}
